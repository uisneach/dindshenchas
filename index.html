        About this map

        The dindshenchas (“lore of places”) is a medieval Irish collection of stories explaining how places got their names, in prose and in verse.

        Texts are from public-domain editions: Whitley Stokes, “The Prose Tales in the Rennes Dindshenchas”, Revue Celtique 15–16 (1894–95), and Edward Gwynn, The Metrical Dindshenchas, Parts I–III (1903–1913) and Poems from the Dindshenchas (1900), via scans at the Internet Archive.

        The original-language text is Middle Irish, the later medieval stage of the language usually grouped with Old Irish.

        Faoin léarscáil seo

        Bailiúchán meánaoiseach Gaeilge is ea an dindshenchas (“seanchas na n-áiteanna”) de scéalta, i bprós agus i véarsaíocht, a mhíníonn conas a fuair áiteanna a n-ainmneacha.

        Is ó eagráin atá i bhfearann an phobail na téacsanna: Whitley Stokes, “The Prose Tales in the Rennes Dindshenchas”, Revue Celtique 15–16 (1894–95), agus Edward Gwynn, The Metrical Dindshenchas, Codanna I–III (1903–1913) agus Poems from the Dindshenchas (1900), trí scananna sa Internet Archive.

        Meán-Ghaeilge atá sa bhuntéacs, an chéim dhéanach mheánaoiseach den teanga, a áirítear go minic leis an tSean-Ghaeilge.
